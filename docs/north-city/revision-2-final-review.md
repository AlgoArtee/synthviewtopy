## Improvements verified in final captures

- The duvet has localized gathered folds among calmer areas and stays in contact with the mattress. The conspicuous hovering rear strip from revision-2 pass 1 is absent.
- The loose pillow has a closed matte edge, reducing the earlier rigid, reflective seam and dark underside gap. It now reads more consistently as fabric.
- The carpet's response is softer, with fewer harsh highlights. It remains an ivory fibrous surface in the correct zone before the bed.
- The arched reveal has continuous illumination without the hard diagonal wedge seen before revision 2. Clear, cloudy, rain, storm, snow and fog captures show distinct atmospheric depth and illumination.
- The bathroom reads as pearl and pastel rather than dark slate. Cyan and pink joints, wall art, shelf and glass planter strengthen the match to reference 3.
- The transparent chair contains its cushions within the bowl. Its floating state is explicitly requested in revision 2 and is assessed as an intentional futuristic design, not an accidental unsupported-object defect.
- The concentric ceiling fitting remains attached and readable from the ceiling and both oblique views. The camera no longer clips through the chair in the ceiling presentation.
- The revised overview includes more of the apartment's functional zoning. Desktop and mobile presentation retain the bedroom, arch, chair and ceiling identity.

## Remaining limitations

1. **Textiles are still the largest realism gap.** The duvet is materially more plausible than in the preceding passes but remains too smooth in broad regions, while fold transitions and the pillow shapes are simpler than actual relaxed cotton. This is the main reason the score is close to the threshold rather than substantially above it.
2. **The rug reads as fine granular or short curled pile.** It lacks the reference's longer, directionally overlapping shag fibers and soft clumped silhouette.
3. **The city is visibly modular.** Repeated facade bays, sign designs, balcony rhythm and window patterns reduce natural variation. Snow, cables, bridges, signs and atmospheric depth add useful detail, but do not eliminate repetition.
4. **Some lighting remains schematic.** Large walls and the enlarged floor have uniform ambient response; colored bathroom highlights are more localized than the reference. Fine contact/AO grain is noticeable on close inspection.
5. **The expanded room is sparsely furnished.** Its circulation is clear and the larger area is requested, but some broad views are dominated by empty floor. No extra furniture was assumed necessary for accepting the specified layout.
6. **Small manufactured details are simplified.** Cabinet edges, chair upholstery, metal fittings and luminous strips have less surface variation and micro-detail than photographed objects.

These limitations are retained in the accepted evaluation. They should not be described as solved or as photographic equivalence.
