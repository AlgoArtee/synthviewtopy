# Time-dependent plaza and skyline flicker

The earlier sparse distant-light animation now covers 31 independent balcony-city materials, 14 north-city materials and eight plaza lanterns. Storefront signs, distant facade accents, small window groups and selected distant billboards receive separate dim-and-recovery events. Each lantern's core, halo, pavement spill and existing pointlight share its local event; other lanterns and pedestrian clothing stay independent.

Night events repeat on seeded 6.5–15.5-second cycles, evening on 7.5–17-second cycles and morning on 8.5–18.5-second cycles. Day uses 19–41-second cycles and much shallower dips. The two brief dips within an event vary with seed and cycle, never brighten above steady emission, and restore steady emission when reduced motion is enabled. The skyline receives stronger modulation than the lanterns. Existing time/weather lighting, sharp non-fog night neon and fog halos are retained.

Animation changes bounded material/light scalars. Existing geometry and textures stay static during flicker; no additional real pointlights or per-frame texture generation were introduced. Small distant window patches reuse cropped areas of their existing facade atlas. This is an implementation cost check, not a new frame-rate benchmark.

The production build passes with the existing large-chunk advisory. Pure sampler validation passes 1,555,416 samples across four times, checking bounds, deterministic independent timing, reduced motion and the weaker daytime profile. The focused actual-room capture in `test-artifacts/north-city/revision-23/final-1b` passes 120 checks, including all four times, all ten weather selections, independent local emission and reduced motion, with ten stills, two native 12-second recordings, no browser errors and unchanged source hashes. Every emitter participated during its 48-second sample at each time. Night's dimmed-sample fraction was approximately 8.5–9.2%, versus 2.8–3.2% by day. Those numbers verify runtime integration; actual temporal images establish visible local changes.

The first diagnostic capture is preserved in `final-1`. Its sole failing assertion demanded too many simultaneously different plaza/day pulses, conflicting with the intentionally rare daytime behavior. The check now requires a dimmed emitter alongside steady neighbors and retains the separate multi-emitter participation check. No scene changes were needed; the corrected capture passes. Exact before/dim/recovery frames show its local emission falling and recovering with no scene changes or browser errors. Its visible change is subtler than nearby storefronts. Reduced size and competing signs can still make distant flicker easy to miss in a single still. Continuous video playback or audio audition is not claimed.

Small far-window footprints and bright lantern cores remain documented limitations.

Proposed project-local lesson: when tuning short light events, capture the actual canvas and matching emission state in the same render step, then inspect local before/dim/recovery images as well as runtime cadence.
