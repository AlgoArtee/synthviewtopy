# Distant-window television shadows

About 40% of distant residential window cells now show independent TV-like light and changing interior shadows. The GPU layer combines slow program color/light changes with a broad moving cast shadow and a soft seated silhouette. It uses the original window maps, preserving interior furniture, curtains and frames. Selection is stable; a room does not repeatedly switch between participating and nonparticipating windows. Each participating room has its own phase.

The balcony skyline contains 24,576 selected cells out of 61,440 tagged distant-facade cells (40.0%). The north skyline and distant street-canyon residential windows contain 599 selected windows out of 1,496 (40.04%). Counts describe modeled windows, including windows hidden by other buildings; the selected proportion visible from any one camera can differ. Near apartment-facing windows and storefronts retain their existing treatment. Some previously dark selected rooms receive low TV illumination, so selection is not limited to the already-bright subset.

Night uses full effect strength, with slightly softer evening and morning light and reduced strength by day. Reduced motion fixes TV light, color and shadow placement at a steady frame. The earlier local neon/sign flicker and weather-dependent lighting remain wired. Existing localized window overlays share the underlying face's TV seed without double-counting. All geometry and texture maps remain static during animation, and no pointlights were added. This is a shader-based distant-room illusion, not additional simulated TV hardware or physical shadow-casting lights.
