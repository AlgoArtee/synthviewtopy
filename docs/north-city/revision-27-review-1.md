## Findings

**The speed control is visually successful.** It has its own clearly labeled Plaza data stream speed panel and a readable `1.0×` value. The desktop panel matches the existing settings, and the mobile image shows the label, track and value without overlap. The native sequence shows bright packets changing position along the stable foreground trunk and plaza branches while the mall and pavement geometry remain fixed. The stills establish readable activity; exact speed ratios, phase continuity, pause behavior, saved values and reduced-motion behavior remain functional checks rather than visual inferences.

**The graphic signs improve the distant light vocabulary.** The tall mint graphic behind the central rail and the wide cyan wave-like board to its right are easily distinguishable in the main night view. Smaller violet and warm accents extend the existing signs, and the mounted vertical detail visible through the north window adds depth without overwhelming the nearby Nova, Luna and Cafe signs. This is a useful improvement over repeated text-only rectangles. The train and rail remain legible above the mall, and the skyline additions do not visibly intrude into the plaza or apartment.

**The promised dimensional skyline forms are still too weakly legible for acceptance of this request.** In the normal main balcony composition, most roof additions are outside the top edge or lost against the dense window field. The northern and southern crown views deliberately expose the roofline, but their dominant impression remains repeated stepped rectangular towers with tiny, nearly dark appendages. A few circular halo accents are recognizable, particularly left of center in the southern view. The uplink, open-lantern, sloped and twin-stack forms are not comparably clear as distinct architectural silhouettes or luminous shapes at this native size. Fine dotted or thin horizontal roof marks read as more small window noise. They can exist correctly in geometry and still fail to make the far city noticeably more interesting to look at.

**The reference's distant light hierarchy has only been partly approached.** Its far district uses conspicuous vertical cyan beacons, broad graphic faces, varied roof masses and occasional magenta or warm landmarks amid a comparatively quieter field of windows. The implementation's new central graphic boards take a step in that direction, but the far skyline is still visually dominated by a nearly uniform carpet of pale residential openings. Much of the new roof detail is lower contrast than those ordinary windows. The requested improvement does not require matching the reference's photorealism or replacing the city; it does require a few clearly readable dimensional neon landmarks at the actual viewing distance.

**Weather integration is coherent but does not resolve the clear-night visibility issue.** The day clear image softens the signs appropriately. Morning cloud, evening fog and snow naturally reduce distant contrast while the closer boards and glazing remain readable. Fog's reduced visibility is plausible and should be retained. The weak clear-night roof forms are the acceptance issue, rather than the intentional obscuration in fog or snow.

## Concrete correction before round 2

1. Make a selected set of supported roof forms large and clear enough to survive the normal 951×892 projection. Favor recognizable open space, thicker structural members and restrained luminous edges over many subpixel or dotted roof details. At least several distinct forms should read in the northern and southern production views without crops or labels.
2. Establish a few distant landmarks with stronger hierarchy than ordinary window rows: for example a readable cyan uplink, a visibly supported lantern or halo, and a sloped or paired technical crown with a different silhouette. Place some within the main balcony framing as well as at the skyline's highest edge. Keep the rail sightline and mall composition clear.
3. Give those selected structures and mounted graphic signs localized cyan, magenta or warm accents that remain legible on clear nights. Avoid brightening the entire window field or outlining every building equally; distributed contrast and distinct shapes are what the reference contributes.
4. Recapture the same native main, northern and southern balcony night views after the correction, plus evening and a day/fog integration check. Preserve the successful speed-control layout and packet motion. Compare the readable landmarks before consulting source counts or geometry preflights.
